# Card: EFN-CONFLICTS — conflicts between sources become notes, never silent adjudication

**Canonical:** [[WP:NPOV#Due and undue weight]], explanatory notes ([[Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Notes]]) — mechanism is house style

## Operative clause

When two ledger-fetched sources conflict on a fact (dates, figures,
characterizations), the article text does NOT silently pick one. State the
resolution options in text if the conflict is significant to the topic, or
attach an explanatory note (`{{efn|…}}` with `{{notelist}}`) recording the
conflicting source and its differing figure. The reader sees the record; the
editor's judgment never erases a published disagreement.

## Load-bearing specifics

- Text carries the mainstream/better-sourced figure; the `{{efn}}` records
  the outlier ("A 1987 profile gives 1919; cemetery records give 1920.").
- Both sources must be in the session ledger with verified quotes.
- If the conflict is *due* (a real scholarly dispute), it may deserve body
  text, not just a note — that's an UNDUE judgment, not this card.

## Failure examples

- Two fetched sources give different founding years; draft states one with
  the other's citation bundled after it (implying both support it) →
  must become text-with-attribution or efn.
- Operator fixture lineage: the "Danish survey fusion" is the sibling
  failure — fusion instead of separated attribution. Fusing is worse than
  adjudicating; separate, attribute, note.
