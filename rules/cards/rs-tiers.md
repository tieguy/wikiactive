# Card: RS-TIERS — reliable sources and their tiers

**Canonical:** [[WP:RS]] (`rules/canonical/reliable-sources.wikitext`), [[WP:RSP]] (`rules/canonical/rsp.wikitext`, seed table in `rules/sources/rsp-seed.tsv`)

## Operative clause

Sources are tiered: (1) peer-reviewed/scholarly publishers and professionally
edited news of record — default for factual claims; (2) reputable trade/
regional/interest press — usually fine, watch self-interest on their own
industry; (3) user-generated, AI-slop, PR, blogs, social — generally unusable
for factual claims, occasionally usable for *descriptive* claims about
themselves (attributed) or for the author's own opinion (attributed as
opinion). RSP precedent controls: check the seed table before registering a
source.

## Load-bearing specifics

- The author's *reputation for fact-checking*, not the brand's logo, is the
  question; "news aggregation" sites have no fact-checking layer.
- Deny-list sources (Grokipedia-class, predatory journals) never anchor
  content. Caution sources (TED talks, personal blogs, extension/vendor
  blogs) only for attributed opinion or self-description.
- Complement-only sources (Find a Grave) support nothing alone — cross-check
  with a tier-1 source or don't use them.

## Failure examples

- Registering a vendor blog as support for the vendor's product's importance.
- Citing a TED talk for a factual claim ("X causes Y") — TED is a lecture
  platform, not fact-checked journalism; attribute as the speaker's view if
  used at all.
